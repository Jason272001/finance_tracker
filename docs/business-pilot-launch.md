# KeeperBMA: first customers before broad promotion

Start with a small owner-led pilot, not a claim to replace every business system. The initial audience should be one group you can reach directly: for example, small independent takeaway shops that want clearer staff handovers and a shared kitchen display. Card processing and full payroll remain outside this first release, so qualify that requirement early.

## First 30 days

1. Speak with 10 business owners or managers. Ask how they handle cashier handovers, kitchen tickets, attendance and daily sales. Watch their existing workflow with permission. Record the problem, frequency and whether they would pay to solve it.
2. Invite 2–3 suitable businesses to a guided pilot. Start with staff access/time tracking or a clearly bounded cash-order workflow. Use real workflows only after the owner completes setup and recovery checks. Do not ask them to replace a working payment system with an unfinished integration.
3. Observe setup without helping immediately. Fix the points where owners hesitate. Measure whether they can create a business, activate staff, pair a device and complete the intended task. Ask whether they used it again during the following week.
4. Resolve serious defects before expanding. Collect permission for a short testimonial or anonymized case study. Never post staff names, timesheets, sales totals or customer details without appropriate permission.
5. Expand through owner referrals and one useful product example at a time. Keep paid advertising at zero until a pilot business uses the product repeatedly and you know which problem earns a response.

## Social accounts

Create one consistent KeeperBMA business profile on Instagram and a Facebook business page if your intended owners use them. Add the website and a clear description. Other channels can wait. Social accounts support trust; direct owner conversations are the main initial acquisition method. Use a separate brand identity from your unrelated projects.

Suggested bio draft:

> Business devices, staff handovers and daily work in one workspace. Owner-led onboarding for small-business pilots. Learn what is available at keeperbma.com.

Suggested content sequence:

- Show one problem: two cashiers should not share an employee identity.
- Show one workflow: cashier records a cash order; the kitchen receives its ticket.
- Show one trust feature: manager approval unlocks management, then locks again.
- Share a genuine pilot result only after obtaining permission and confirming the result.

Do not advertise live card processing, automated payroll, tax filing or a full accounting suite. Keep the current walkthrough clearly identified as using fictional data; update it only when the feature set is settled, as requested.

## Direct invitation draft

> Hi [name], I’m building KeeperBMA for small businesses. It separates the owner account from staff access and connects cashier, kitchen and time-clock devices. I’m looking for a few owners willing to show me how they handle staff handovers and orders. Could I ask you a few questions? If there’s a fit, I can show the working tools and help with a small pilot. Card processing and full payroll are not included yet.

Send invitations personally only to appropriate contacts; do not mass-message or post automatically. Confirm that the advertised support inbox is actually receiving mail before using it as a public contact channel.

## Revenue target

At the currently published Business list price of $25/month, 4 paying businesses equal $100/month and 8 equal $200/month in gross subscription revenue. Hosting, payment fees, support and other expenses reduce the amount retained. This is arithmetic, not a prediction or a guarantee of customers. First establish repeat use and willingness to pay.
